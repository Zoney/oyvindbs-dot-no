EXILE AT FORTY

The Homborsund Saga

An eight-part symphonic power-metal epic about a man who is, at the time of this writing, still thirty-nine.

So. What is this.

It's a real album. That's the joke.

Somewhere between a birthday card and a minor act of aggression, this is a full studio album. Eight songs. Forty minutes. Double kick drums, a choir that will not be reasoned with, keyboards that clearly attended a Finnish conservatory, and a tenor who believes, sincerely, in you.

It lives on Spotify, Apple Music, Tidal, YouTube — every store that would have us. A stranger in Ohio can, at this very moment, accidentally stream a five-minute power ballad about your specific personality and have no idea what they've done.

It is not a joke. The joke is that it is not a joke.

For whom, exactly

For Eivind Andreassen-Rosmer.

Son of the coast. Born in Kilsund, keeper of Homborsund, tamer of things that would kill a lesser man on contact. A friend of the kind that answers the phone when the phone should not, by any reasonable standard, be answered.

The album casts him, with zero restraint and full orchestra, as a returning hero — the Wanderer, the Guardian, the man the whole coastline sings for. He did not ask for this. That is rather the point.

(This page lives at /ean. Eivind Andreassen-… you can see where that's going. Also, coincidentally, an international barcode standard — fitting, for a man now available as a scannable product.)

What the songs are about

We will not be telling you which is which.

There are eight chapters. Consider the following less a tracklist and more a diagnostic. You'll recognise more of yourself than is strictly comfortable. Which clue is which song — that, you'll have to earn by pressing play.

  • A certain small place by the sea, and forty winters a man wore like a good coat.
  • A forbidden realm, entered nightly at an hour no honest person defends, where treasure is eternally promised and the phrase "just one more" is the only known prayer.
  • A sacrament of two men, performed on foot, in the cold, involving a specific and holy quantity of grilled meat.
  • A great feat of endurance — meticulously planned, then undone the night before by an affliction with no fever and suspiciously excellent supporting telemetry.
  • The domestication of lightning and copper, by a man who does not flinch.
  • One offhand sentence about camels, which turned out to be an entire philosophy of life.
  • A house by the water. Warm laughter. Two sons, one very patient wife, and a door that is never quite locked.

That's seven. The eighth is the one where everyone raises the torches. You'll know it when it lands on you.
